Spending in retirement

We assume you both start drawing retirement income together from the later of your two retirement ages, and plan through to the later of your two life expectancies. Each of you takes a 25% tax-free lump sum from your pension at that point (up to the pension lump sum allowance). To meet your share of the household target each year, we draw first from your pension (taxed as income under your own Personal Allowance and bands, with Marriage Allowance applied automatically where enabled), then — if that's not enough — from your GIA (Capital Gains Tax on the gain portion of each withdrawal), then finally your ISA (tax-free). Your GIA also pays annual dividend tax on assumed dividend income each year, in and out of retirement.

Simulation settings

Instead of a single fixed rate, each simulated year draws a random investment return and a random inflation rate from a normal distribution around your expected values (set on the Overview tab). This shows a spread of possible outcomes rather than one estimate. We assume no correlation between returns and inflation, and each year is independent of the last — real markets have more structure than this.

This tool gives rough, educational estimates using simplified compounding and simplified UK Income Tax, pension annual allowance and Marriage Allowance rules (England, Wales & Northern Ireland rates) — real tax law has more detail (National Insurance, student loans, benefits, Scottish rates, exact HMRC definitions of threshold/adjusted income, phased tax-free cash, and more). It isn't financial, tax, or legal advice. Consider speaking with a licensed financial adviser or accountant for decisions specific to your situation.
